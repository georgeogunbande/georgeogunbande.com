Why this book

Kids learn reading, maths and science. Who teaches them to build?

Most children never hear how a business starts until they’re adults. By then, many have decided it’s something other people do.

Maya the Mini Mogul changes that early. Through one girl’s story, young readers see that a business is just a useful idea, tried out carefully and improved with every customer. They also see that getting it wrong is part of getting it right.

Every big company started as somebody’s small idea.

What kids learn

Five chapters. Five founder habits.

Each chapter builds one habit, told through Maya’s adventure rather than a lecture.

Habit

Spot the problem

Notice what people around you need or keep complaining about.

Habit

Start small

Test the idea with what you already have before spending anything.

Habit

Know your numbers

Understand what things cost, what to charge and what’s left over.

Habit

Learn from mistakes

Treat a setback as information, then try again a little smarter.

Habit

Bring people along

Work with friends and family, and treat customers kindly.

Questions

For parents & educators.

Is it right for a six-year-old?

Younger readers enjoy it as a read-aloud with a parent. Most children from about eight can read it on their own.

Does it push kids to make money?

No. It teaches problem-solving, effort, honesty and how to treat people. Money shows up as one practical tool, not the goal.
